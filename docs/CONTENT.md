## Scientific corrections and teacher support

- Most mammals bear live young. Platypuses and echidnas lay eggs. Do not teach live birth as an exceptionless defining trait. [Natural History Museum](https://www.nhm.ac.uk/discover/the-platypus-puzzle.html).
- Mammals breathe with lungs, produce milk for young, and have hair at some life stage. A whale may have very little visible hair. Warm-blooded is a supported discussion concept, not an extra picture-word count. [NOAA marine mammals](https://www.noaa.gov/education/resource-collections/marine-life/marine-mammals).
- Dolphins are mammals and breathe air; fish use gills to take oxygen from water. Air-breathing alone does not define mammals. Fish also have backbones. [NOAA dolphin facts](https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/facts/dolphin.html).
- The source lists “ocean floor” as a habitat. Marine mammals may feed near the bottom but must return to air. Do not imply underwater breathing.
- Lungs, backbone and gills require simple non-graphic diagrams. Blowhole, tusk/horn/antlers, pouch, echidna and platypus need adult explanation and pronunciation support. Porpoise and dugong are extensions to compare with more familiar animals.
- Land/water categories overlap for many animals, so Sort It sorts word roles: mammal names, fish names, bodies, places/food, actions, and names for young. A kitten is also a mammal, but the task is its specific field-guide section, not denying this relationship. Explain this explicitly to children.
- Hop and jump are related actions: hop uses repeated small jumps; the selected pictures contrast rabbit hopping and a single cat jump. Feed and drink have different agents in their picture briefs. Hide is an action; den/burrow/cave are shelter words with different scopes, not interchangeable aliases.
