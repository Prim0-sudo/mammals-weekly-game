## What stays familiar

- A complete illustrated world fills the screen and continues behind the menu and activity panels.
- A dominant central illustrated topic identity is the single entry point.
- The same eight activity cards and familiar navigation; no menu before entry.
- Each card has a recognisable picture, a clear title and one short description.
- Cream/light panels and large isolated learning pictures provide readable contrast against a detailed world.
- Topic colours, badge shapes, scenery and spelling characters change together. The game family stays recognisable without reusing another topic's actual scenery.
- Later engine fixes and teacher controls remain; visual continuity does not justify reverting their behaviour.

## Why the rejected draft fails

The plain gradient, oversized editorial heading, numbered text cards, field-guide numbering and boxed missing-picture descriptions remove the illustrated learning experience. A text-based Field Notes panel is not visually comparable to the previous character-led spelling games. These are missing design components, not small colour or border adjustments.
